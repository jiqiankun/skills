# Photography Principles

Use this reference to resolve tensions between valid photographic goals. Do not treat every maxim as an absolute processing order.

## Conflict priority

Make later priorities yield to earlier ones:

1. photographic truth, identity, and original semantic content;
2. explicit user intent and the requested subject hierarchy, within the hard contract;
3. source evidence about light, material, atmosphere, and scene logic;
4. subject-background harmony;
5. exposure, white-balance, dynamic-range, lens, and sensor correction;
6. the selected photographic style;
7. cosmetic polish and micro-detail.

## Hard principles

- **Preserve Truth:** retain the factual basis of people, place, light, weather, material, and time of capture.
- **Identity Preservation:** improve presentation without changing facial geometry, body structure, permanent features, or individual skin character.
- **Pixel Editing, Not Regeneration:** adjust captured pixels; never synthesize missing content or detail.
- **Preserve Meaningful Texture:** retain pores, fabric weave, food texture, masonry, foliage, water, mist, and other source-supported material cues.
- **Lighting Coherence:** keep source direction, softness, falloff, color source, occlusion, and subject-background relationship physically coherent.
- **Verify After Editing:** treat the render as untrusted until visual and metric checks pass.

## Default workflow principles

- **Distinguish Correction from Intent:** correct capture errors before styling, but preserve intentional warmth, silhouettes, haze, high key, low key, and motivated color.
- **Whole Before Parts:** understand the narrative and frame before optimizing a face, sky, building, or garment.
- **Narrative Hierarchy First:** serve the actual primary subject; a person in a landscape may be scale, context, or co-subject rather than the automatic lead.
- **Tonal Hierarchy Before Creative Palette:** establish readable light structure before making expressive palette decisions. Do not interpret this as a literal low-level color-pipeline order.
- **Global Foundation, Local Restraint:** establish a moderate global base, then correct isolated issues locally instead of using aggressive global changes.
- **Color Relationships:** tune relative hue, chroma, and luminance among skin, vegetation, sky, architecture, garments, and water instead of merely raising saturation.
- **Source Responsive:** derive every numeric recipe from the current source. A named style defines goals and limits, not fixed parameters.
- **Restraint:** prefer a result that looks well photographed. Strong style is allowed only when requested and still yields to the hard principles.

## Conditional principles

- **Skin Before Palette** applies only when a person is primary or co-primary and the face has enough pixels. Preserve plausible environmental color on skin under sunset, stage, neon, or reflected light.
- **Local correction** applies only after the global base. Local-first processing can be invalidated by later global changes and can create halos or pasted-on subjects.
- **Texture protection** does not mean sharpening noise, bokeh, atmospheric haze, or genuinely soft focus.
- **Reference matching** yields to source light, skin, protected colors, and recoverable information.
- **Uncertainty means restraint:** if a mark may be permanent, an object may be meaningful, or a mask is unreliable, leave it unchanged.
